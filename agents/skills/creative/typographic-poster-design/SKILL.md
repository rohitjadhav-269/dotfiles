# Typographic Poster Design

## Procedure

1. **Make a copy contract before composing.**
   - Transcribe supplied copy exactly: capitalization, punctuation, numbering, and deliberate anomalies are content.
   - Treat a request to “take text from” an image as a constraint against inventing slogan, metadata, header, footer, or caption copy.
   - Study any visual reference for transferable principles—hierarchy, density, scale, palette, rhythm—not its distinctive layout or assets.

2. **Build the hierarchy before decoration.**
   - Give the headline a generous protected field inside the canvas; judge clearance from the actual glyph bounds, not its baseline or text box.
   - Use a small number of typographic levels and alignment rules. Let dividers, color fields, or blank space organize the reading order instead of adding explanatory microcopy.
   - Treat large negative space as a positive compositional element. When a headline leads into a list, leave a deliberate, visually generous interval before the list begins.
   - When the user wants the source list to sit lower, anchor it toward the lower half or lower third while retaining intentional bottom clearance.

3. **Set type with optical, not merely numeric, spacing.**
   - Default to sentence or title case when requested; never use all caps as a substitute for hierarchy.
   - Tighten tracking and leading in separate adjustments. Keep a visible air gap between adjacent glyph shapes and between successive lines—tightness must never rely on collisions or overlap.
   - Test large headline letterforms with their actual ascenders, descenders, counters, and side bearings; perceived contact often differs from bounding-box contact.
   - Preserve source punctuation in display type, including unusual numbering labels such as a spelled-out ordinal.

4. **Refine from the rendered artifact.**
   - Render at final dimensions, inspect the full canvas, then correct only the observed issue: margins, leading, tracking, visual weight, or content clutter.
   - Remove nonessential header, footer, badge, and annotation copy when it dilutes the supplied text or the user asks for a quieter composition.
   - Keep each revision surgical: reposition the affected hierarchy or whitespace region rather than adding new decorations.

5. **Preflight before delivery.**
   - Verify every source line is present, readable, unclipped, and not collision-obscured.
   - Check headline clearance on all surrounding edges; inspect at least the top, left/right, and the transition into the body content.
   - Confirm all type uses the requested casing and that tight spacing remains visibly non-overlapping.
   - Deliver the final downloadable render first; include process material only when asked.

## Pitfalls

- Do not add metadata-style filler to make a poster feel designed; unsupported microcopy competes with the supplied message and is the first element users ask to remove.
- Do not infer margin safety from an SVG text baseline or CSS box; oversized glyphs can visually crowd a rule or border despite mathematically valid coordinates.
- Do not solve crowded headline-to-list transitions by shrinking the headline alone; establish a meaningful negative-space interval before weakening the primary hierarchy.
- Do not treat tight tracking or leading as permission for contact; optical separation is the non-negotiable boundary between purposeful density and a rendering defect.
